Path to licensure

From LLMSW to LMSW in Michigan

The Michigan Department of Licensing and Regulatory Affairs (LARA) sets the steps. This is the path in order, using LARA's own hour counts.

Adding a second specialty. A clinical or macro designation on top of your first takes another 2,000 post-degree hours over at least one year, including at least 50 hours of supervisory review, plus the exam for that specialty.

Supervision by video. Anne's supervision meets live over secure video. LARA's rule allows live video for the individual portion.

  1. Hold an active limited license

    After your master's degree in social work, you practice under a Michigan limited license (LLMSW). Supervised experience counts only while that license is active.

  2. Work under a qualified supervisor

    Your supervisor must be a Michigan-licensed master social worker in good standing, holding the clinical or macro designation that matches your specialty.

  3. Complete 4,000 supervised hours

    These are post-degree hours earned over at least two years. You average 16 to 40 hours of work per week, and no more than 2,080 hours count in any 12 months.

  4. Receive regular supervisory review

    Supervision totals at least 4 hours per month, and at least 2 of those hours are one-on-one, in person or live over video. Group supervision is allowed when individual contact makes up half of it.

  5. Have your hours verified

    Each supervisor completes LARA's Supervisor's Verification of Social Work Experience form, listing your total hours and your start and end dates.

  6. Pass the exam and apply for the LMSW

    Take the ASWB exam LARA requires, then apply through LARA's MiPLUS system with your transcripts, verification forms, exam scores, and the other items LARA lists.

Requirements come from Michigan's administrative rules and LARA's licensing guide, and they can change. Confirm current details with LARA before you plan your hours. Last reviewed November 2026.
